The Prologue

"Across the vast expanse of the Indian subcontinent, two shadows loomed large... tracing the divine spirits of courage, tactical brilliance, and the undying thirst for freedom."

This study explores the biography, strategy, and contributions of two titans who confronted the Mughal Empire. It builds towards a more comprehensive exploration of their lives, their leadership qualities, and their military genius. Both leaders, though geographically distant, were bound by a common thread of resistance.

Scroll I: The Maratha Narrative

Chhatrapati
Shivaji Maharaj

Early Life and Beginnings

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was born in 1630 at Shivneri Fort near Pune, Maharashtra, to Shahaji Bhosale, a Maratha general, and Jijabai, a woman of immense wisdom and determination.

From a young age, Shivaji was exposed to military training, statecraft, and the harsh realities of the political landscape in the Deccan, under the shadow of the Mughal Empire and the Deccan Sultanates.

Shivaji’s early life was marked by his independence of spirit, particularly evident when, at the age of 16, he seized his first fort, Torna Fort, marking the beginning of his ambition to establish a Maratha kingdom.

Foundation of the Empire

Shivaji's primary objective was not only to build a kingdom but also to liberate the Hindavi Swarajya (indigenous rule) from foreign oppression. He relentlessly fought against the Mughals, Bijapur Sultanate, and Golkonda Sultanate while building alliances with local powers.

By the 1640s, he had already carved out a formidable domain along the western coast, including Raigad, which he made his capital. His expertise in fort warfare, guerrilla tactics, and naval strategy gradually built a state that defied the prevailing Mughal power.

The Milestones of Valour

1645 Torna Fort

The first military victory that announced the arrival of a sovereign spirit.

1665 Purandar Siege

A strategic confrontation where he surrendered forts only to reclaim his spirit twice over.

1666 Agra Defiance

A daring escape from the custody of Aurangzeb, becoming an immortal symbol of freedom.

1674 The Coronation

Declared the Chhatrapati at Raigad Fort, formally marking the birth of the Maratha Empire.

Scroll II: The Ahom Narrative

Lachit
Borphukan

Noble Beginnings

Lachit Borphukan was born in 1622 in the Ahom Kingdom. He belonged to the noble Borphukan family, which had a history of military leadership.

His early life was marked by his education in warfare, statecraft, and the dominant culture of the Ahom Kingdom, which was a dominant power in the Brahmaputra valley.

In the mid-1600s, as tensions with the Mughal Empire escalated, he was appointed Borphukan (commander-in-chief), an office of significant military authority.

The Mughal Adversity

The Mughals under Aurangzeb sought to expand eastward into the Brahmaputra valley. The Ahoms, led by Lachit, were caught in a struggle for survival as Assam was a major economic hub.

Lachit’s leadership disrupted Mughal plans for territorial expansion in the northeast. His resistance preserved the sovereignty of Assam against repeated invasions.

SARAIGHAT: 1671

"Faced with an overwhelming Mughal force, Lachit demonstrated unparalleled tactical genius. Despite a life-threatening illness, he personally led his troops and defeated the Mughal forces."

This battle marked the turning point in Assam’s history. Lachit’s strategy focused on riverine warfare, using the terrain of the Brahmaputra to his advantage. His victory ensured that Assam remained free from Mughal control, preserving the Ahom Kingdom’s sovereignty for decades.

Doctrines of Valour

The Maratha Swarajya

Naval Supremacy

Innovations in naval warfare along the Western Ghats protected the empire from Mughal naval threats and European colonizers.

Siege of Surat

A bold raid on a wealthy Mughal port city, sending a powerful message that the Marathas were not easily subdued.

The Ahom Sovereignty

Riverine Tactfulness

The Mughals sought to control the Brahmaputra River valley; Lachit’s fortified defenses thwarted their invasions at every turn.

Moral Fortitude

His commitment to his people and his selflessness in ensuring the safety of Assam made him an immortal figure in history.

Final Synthesis

"Both Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and Lachit Borphukan left behind legacies that transcend regional boundaries. Their stories form the bedrock of Indian resistance and are celebrated in the larger narrative of India’s fight for autonomy."
